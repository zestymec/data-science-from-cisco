§ 1

Everything we do creates data

Every form we fill in, every post we publish and every click we make is recorded somewhere. We are more connected than ever, so every place we go and everything we do is a new source of data. With this volume, organisations can track, isolate and predict behaviour.

Data is described by how it was collected:

Volunteered data
Shared on purpose: sign-up forms, surveys, profiles, posts.
Observed data
Captured by watching behaviour: clicks, location, purchases, device usage.
Inferred data
Derived from other data: predicted interests, credit scores, recommendations.

Why it matters: the way data was collected affects how accurate it is, and how careful we must be about privacy and ethics.

§ 2

Types of data: count it or measure it

Data is quantitative (numbers) or qualitative (descriptions, categories). Quantitative data splits again:

  • Discrete data is collected by counting. Whole numbers only: you cannot sell 2.5 bicycles.
  • Continuous data is collected by measuring. Any value in a range is possible: 36.6 °C, 36.61 °C and so on.

§ 3

Structured vs unstructured data

Structured data fits neatly in rows and columns and lives in relational databases (queried with SQL). Unstructured data, such as photos, video, emails and posts, has no fixed shape and is often kept in non-relational (NoSQL) databases or a data lake. JSON and XML sit in between: they carry keys or tags but no rigid table, so they are called semi-structured.

§ 4

Data visualization

A chart turns raw numbers into a shape the eye can read, so trends and patterns stand out. The skill is matching the chart to the question. Not every chart fits every dataset.

Four factors when choosing a chart

FactorQuestion to ask
Number of variablesHow many characteristics am I measuring?
Number of data pointsHow much information is in each variable?
TimeDoes the data track change (hourly, daily, yearly)?
RelationshipsDo I need to compare or correlate points?
§ 5

Big data and the four Vs

Big data is not just "a lot of data". Its characteristics change how data is collected, sent, stored and accessed, and each V is a challenge for the engineers who build the infrastructure.

Volume
How much data. Needs scalable, distributed storage.
Velocity
How fast it arrives and must be processed.
Variety
Many formats and many sources at once.
Veracity
How trustworthy or uncertain it is.

Benefits of data growth

  • Healthcare: earlier detection and personalised treatment.
  • Retail: demand forecasts and recommendations.
  • Transport: live routing and predictive maintenance.
  • Agriculture and climate: better yields and early warnings.

Veracity is the V people forget. Bad data in means bad decisions out.

§ 6

Managing big data: the pipeline

A data pipeline moves data reliably from where it is created to where it is used. It has three phases: ingestion, transformation and storage.

Data scientists spend a big part of their time in the transformation (cleaning) phase, not in the modelling phase.

File formats: CSV, JSON, XML

§ 8

Artificial intelligence and machine learning

AI is the broad goal of machines doing tasks that normally need human intelligence. Machine learning (ML) is the part of AI where algorithms learn patterns from data instead of following rules someone wrote by hand. Deep learning is a further subset of ML that uses many-layered neural networks. A classifier is an algorithm that sorts new items into categories.

§ 9

Experience analytics

Operational numbers such as sales or logins say what happened. Experience analytics adds the why by collecting how people felt about a product or service and joining that to the operational data. The goal is to find the moments that make customers stay or leave and fix them.

Where experience data comes from

  • Asked directly: surveys, feedback forms, ratings.
  • Written down: reviews, support tickets, social posts and chat logs. This is unstructured text, so it needs text analysis such as sentiment scoring.
  • Observed: clickstreams, time on page, drop-off points, repeat visits.

Common measures

NPS · Net Promoter Score
"How likely are you to recommend us, 0 to 10?" Promoters (9–10) minus detractors (0–6), as a percentage of all answers. Passives (7–8) only count in the total.
CSAT · Customer Satisfaction
Share of customers who rate an interaction as satisfied.
Churn
Share of customers who leave in a period.

The loop

  1. Ask a clear question (why are people abandoning checkout?).
  2. Collect experience and operational data together.
  3. Clean and join them in the pipeline from §6.
  4. Analyse and visualise, choosing charts as in §4.
  5. Act on it, then measure again to see if it worked.

§ 10

Careers in data

Roles in the data analytics professions

RoleMostly works on

Data engineerBuilds and looks after the pipelines and storage that deliver data (the §6 side).
Data scientistBuilds statistical and machine learning models to predict and explain (the §8 side).

The job market

The uses for data, and especially big data, are multiplying fast. A FinancesOnline report (2022) notes that 52% of businesses worldwide see data analytics and predictive analytics as primary components of their operations. Because businesses generate, store, sell and share more data than ever, the market for people who interpret and transform it keeps growing.

According to the World Economic Forum Future of Jobs survey (2020), the three jobs with the highest increase in demand were data analysts and scientists, AI and machine learning specialists, and big data specialists. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects at least 22% more openings in the field by 2030. Industries expected to grow employment in it by at least 50% include motor vehicle manufacturing, online retail and healthcare-related business.

These percentages come from the course text. Search for the original reports before quoting them anywhere else.

Tools and skills for entry-level roles

Skills that job adverts for entry-level positions keep asking for:

  • Attention to detail.
  • Solid verbal and written communication.
  • Ability to work in a team.
  • Spreadsheets such as Excel.
  • SQL and databases.
  • Some experience with object-oriented languages such as Python and Java.
  • Visualization tools and presenting results.

Why a project portfolio matters

A portfolio shows what you can do, not only what you studied. For data work it can be a few notebooks or dashboards with a clear question, the cleaning steps, the chart and the conclusion. These notes are one piece of mine; the rest is on my portfolio and GitHub.
